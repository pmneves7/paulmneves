Alternative twin domains

Twins the lattice permits for this space group. Obverse/reverse twins fill in otherwise-absent reflections; merohedral twins keep the same reflection conditions but overlap reflections (biasing intensities). Pseudo-merohedral twins from accidental metric coincidences are not considered.

    Scattering angle definitions

    When a scattering plane is provided, ω is the smallest signed crystal rotation about the out-of-plane axis that satisfies cos(φ + ω) = −sin(θ)/cos(χ), including both symmetric detector branches (φ = in-plane angle, χ = out-of-plane angle, θ = Bragg angle).

    Intensity

    The integrated estimate needs only illuminated mass and flux per area, plus the loaded crystal and wavelength. It assumes ideal complete collection: ∫R(ω)dω in s⁻¹ rad for a single crystal, or a full-ring rate in s⁻¹ for randomly oriented powder. Mosaicity and beam divergence spread the signal without changing this integral when the whole reflection is captured. A total beam rate also requires its area at the sample; the beam is assumed uniform. The rate column applies optional detector/transmission corrections (blank means 1). For single crystals it additionally requires a full rocking interval to give the mean rate over an equatorial ω scan; collection is the fraction of the Bragg beam captured. For powder, collection is the fraction of the complete Debye ring captured. These are kinematic estimates for a fully illuminated sample, using your mean transmission and detector efficiency; they omit extinction and multiple scattering. X-rays assume unpolarized radiation. The normalization follows the ILL Neutron Data Booklet, §2.9.4.

    X-ray factors use tabulated neutral-atom Cromer–Mann coefficients (0 ≤ sinθ/λ ≤ 2 Å⁻¹), with no anomalous dispersion or Debye–Waller factor. Neutrons use coherent scattering lengths. Atomic phase factors can give a nonzero F_imag for either source. Missing data are flagged. Powder rows sum |F|² over complete symmetry families and coincident d-spacings; F columns and angles refer to the first listed HKL. Display filters do not change that sum.

    Magnetic order estimator

    Simple collinear estimate for neutron satellites at τ ± k. Moments are taken equal and in phase on the selected site or element; the intensity includes a 2/3 orientation average.

    Scattering plane

    Enter two non-collinear HKLs in the scattering plane to calculate a signed out-of-plane angle. The in-plane angle is measured from the first HKL toward the second. HKL 1 defines the in-plane reference axis used for φ and ω (not the incident beam direction).
